## Section 2 — The pattern Jokuh refuses

**Headline:**
Encrypted today. Forgotten tomorrow.

**Body (90 words):**
Mainstream encrypted messengers protect the wire and abandon the message. The conversation evaporates. You search for something said three months ago and find a screen of nothing. Then you go to a worse app — one with no encryption — because at least it remembers.

Messages refuses both compromises. The wire is end-to-end encrypted. The thread persists in your Spine, where only you and the people you addressed can read it. Your AI can search it. Mainstream surveillance cannot.

---

## Section 3 — What it does

**Three feature cards:**

1. **End-to-end encrypted threads**
   Every message is encrypted on the sender's device and decrypted only on the recipient's device. The server forwards but cannot read.

2. **Persistent across devices**
   Your Spine syncs encrypted thread data across the surfaces you use Jokuh on. New device, full history. Only you can decrypt.

3. **Multimodal payloads**
   Text, voice memos, files, payments, blurbs, profile cards. Every payload is encrypted in transit and at rest in Spine.

## Section 4 — Privacy posture (anchor: `#privacy`)

**Body (100 words):**
Messages inherits Spine's confidentiality stack. Keys are generated and held on your device. The Jokuh server cannot see contents, attachments, or metadata about who is talking with whom beyond what the protocol requires for delivery.

When your agent operates on a message — to summarize, search, or respond on your behalf — the operation runs inside a Trusted Execution Environment. Plaintext exists only for the lifetime of the computation, then is sealed back into Spine.

You can forget a thread. Forgetting destroys the keys. The data becomes unrecoverable, by design.

## Section 5 — What threads can do that ordinary messages can't

- **Recall** — ask your Sidekick what a contact said about a topic across every thread you've shared.
